<strong>Five days ago, I told you I have a Master's in software technology, cannot code, and broke into business analysis anyway. Here is the whole way in, on one page, and the part nobody says out loud.</strong> This week walked through what the role really is, the experience you are already carrying, and the practical path to a first job. Today I want to leave you with two things: the honest truths I wish someone had told me at the start, and a simple roadmap you can keep and come back to. I am putting the truths first on purpose, because the thing that stops most people is not a missing skill. It is a quiet, wrong belief about themselves, and the truths below are aimed straight at those beliefs.

## The honest truths nobody tells you

1. It may take longer than you want. The first role is by far the hardest to get. The entrance is the difficult part, and the career itself is easier than the door.
2. You will feel like a fraud, especially early. Everyone does. The imposter feeling is not evidence that you do not belong. It is just what learning something new feels like from the inside.
3. You do not need permission. No one is going to knock and hand you the title. You start behaving like an analyst, producing the work of one, and the title catches up to what you are already doing.
4. Your background is an asset, not an apology. The thing you are afraid disqualifies you is very often the thing that will set you apart from everyone with the same tidy, identical CV.
5. Doing beats collecting. Courses and certificates can help, but the people who get in are the ones who produced something real, not the ones who waited until they had one more badge.
6. Progress is not linear. You will have weeks where it all feels hopeless and weeks where three things move at once. Judge yourself by whether you are still taking steps, not by how fast the door opens.
7. Comparison will lie to you. Someone else's fast entry says nothing about your path. You are not behind. You are on your own clock, and the only pace that matters is that you keep moving.

## The roadmap on one page

Here is the whole path from this week, in the order to walk it.

- First, learn the craft properly, through a course or structured study, until you can speak the language and do the fundamentals.
- Second, do the work before you have the title, by analysing a real process and producing the artefacts an analyst would.
- Third, move sideways where you can, getting close to the analysis work inside a company that already trusts you.
- Fourth, find one person a step ahead to guide you, and actually ask them.
- Fifth, turn your transferable experience, your learning, and your real work into one honest story that shows you can already do the job.

Keep this beside you, and treat it as a sequence of small moves rather than one impossible leap. Read down that list and notice something reassuring: not a single step on it needs a job you do not yet have. Every one is available to you today, which means the path is not something you wait to be let onto. It is something you start walking now.

None of these steps requires anyone's permission, and that is the whole point. Every one of them is something you can begin this week, from exactly where you are, with exactly what you already have.

If you can only take one thing from the roadmap, take this: do the work before you have the title. Everything else supports it, and it is the step almost everyone skips, because producing something real and risking a judgement feels far more exposing than collecting one more course. But it is the move that breaks the catch-22, because it turns you from someone asking to be given a chance into someone who can already show the work. <strong><em>Aspiration is invisible. A process you mapped and improved is not.</em></strong> Make the invisible thing visible, and the conversation with any employer changes completely.

If all of this feels like a lot, shrink it to one action. This coming week, do a single thing that moves you forward. Enrol in a course, or map one broken process at your current job and write it up, or send one message to a person a step ahead asking whether they would talk to you for twenty minutes. Just one. Momentum here is not built by giant leaps. It is built by small, repeated proof that you are serious, first to other people, and more importantly to yourself. The ones who break in are simply the ones who kept taking the next small step after the last one.

## One last thing

Someone did all of this for me once. When I doubted whether an engineer who could not code had any place in tech, a mentor guided me in and reminded me that I belonged. Everything I have written this week is, in a real sense, me passing that on, because the help I was given was never truly mine to keep. So if you take one thing from these five days, let it be this: you belong in this field more than the fear in your head will admit, and the way in is not a locked door. It is a craft you can learn and a series of small, brave moves you can begin this week. Start with one. Then take the next. That is how all of us got in, and it is how you will too. Thank you for spending the week with me. Now go and take the first step. Wherever you are reading this, whatever your background, whatever you were once told you could not do, there is a place for you in this field, and the only permission you have been waiting for is your own.

<strong><em>Go out and be successful.</em></strong>
