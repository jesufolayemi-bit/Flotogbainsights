In yesterday's discussion, we introduced the concept of business processes, highlighting their importance in improving efficiency, productivity, customer satisfaction, and decision-making. Today, we'll discuss characteristics of business processes, identifying them, and touch the role of BAs in managing these processes.

As defined yesterday, a business process is a collection of interconnected activities or tasks that are carried out by people or equipment in a specific order to produce a service or product that meets a particular business goal for one or more customers.

## Key Characteristics of Business Processes

1. <strong><em>Purposeful</em></strong>: Each process is designed to achieve a specific business goal or objective.
2. <strong><em>Structured</em></strong>: Processes, ideally follow a set sequence of activities or steps.
3. <strong><em>Repeatable</em></strong>: They can be executed multiple times with consistent results.
4. <strong><em>Measurable</em></strong>: Performance metrics can be applied to evaluate effectiveness and efficiency.

## Examples of Business Processes

1. <strong><em>Order-to-Cash</em></strong>: Encompasses all activities from receiving a customer order to collecting payment.
2. <strong><em>Procure-to-Pay</em></strong>: Covers the procurement cycle from supplier selection to payment.
3. <strong><em>Hire-to-retire</em></strong>: Covers the human capital cycle of an employee from joining the organisation to exiting the organisation.
4. <strong><em>Customer Service</em></strong>: Involves activities from handling customer inquiries to resolving issues.

Identifying business processes within an organization is a critical step towards understanding and improving them. This involves recognizing the various activities and workflows that contribute to the organization’s goals.

## Techniques for Identifying Business Processes

1. <strong><em>Process Mapping</em></strong>: Visual representation of processes using flowcharts or diagrams to map out each step and decision point.
2. <strong><em>Stakeholder Interviews</em></strong>: Engaging with employees, managers, and other stakeholders to gather insights into daily operations and workflows.
3. <strong><em>Document Analysis</em></strong>: Reviewing existing documentation, such as standard operating procedures (SOPs), manuals, and policy documents.
4. <strong><em>Observation</em></strong>: Directly observing the work environment to see how tasks are performed and identify any informal processes.

## Steps in Identifying Business Processes

1. <strong><em>Define Objectives</em></strong>: Clearly state the purpose of identifying and analyzing business processes.
2. <strong><em>Gather Information</em></strong>: Collect data through interviews, observations, and document reviews.
3. <strong><em>Map Processes</em></strong>: Create visual representations of processes to understand the flow of activities.
4. <strong><em>Validate Findings</em></strong>: Confirm the accuracy of process maps with stakeholders.
